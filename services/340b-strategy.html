When this is useful

Move beyond a roster of pharmacies and toward a strategy.

Growth

Capture is below potential

Prescribing volume exists, but referral destinations, payer restrictions, or pharmacy coverage keep value outside the network.

Complexity

The system has multiple entities

Covered entities, owned pharmacies, contract pharmacies, and service lines need to be viewed as one operating footprint without losing entity-level context.

Decision

Priorities are unclear

Leadership needs to know whether to optimize the current network, add capacity, pursue designations, or focus on a different opportunity first.

What the engagement examines

A connected view of opportunity, coverage, and constraints.

01 / OPPORTUNITY

Prescribing and referral opportunity

Size specialty and retail opportunity from available prescribing data, with drug, prescriber, site, payer, and destination views where the source supports them.

02 / FOOTPRINT

Owned and contract-pharmacy coverage

Compare destination patterns with owned pharmacies and the relevant OPAIS contract-pharmacy roster to identify in-house, contracted, and uncovered opportunity.

03 / ACCESS

Addressable-market constraints

Separate headline opportunity from the portion constrained by payer networks, limited distribution, entity type, and other documented assumptions.

04 / ACTION

Prioritized capture plan

Translate analytical gaps into a practical sequence of network, referral, data, and operating actions with explicit assumptions and limitations.

What you receive

Important boundary

Analysis supports—but does not replace—program compliance review.

Manufacturer policies, state-law effects, entity attribution, and reference data change over time. Recommendations identify the reference basis and where legal, compliance, or operational confirmation is still required.
